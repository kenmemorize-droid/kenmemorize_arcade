CIRCUIT CASCADE

Wire the chips so every bulb burns red

How to Play

Power runs left to right. Red is a live signal, yellow is dead, and grey means an empty slot is blocking the wire.

Tap a chip in the tray, then tap a slot to drop it in. Each chip rewrites the two lines passing through it, and the wires recolour as you go.

Fill every slot and get red into both inputs of every bulb. Light them all and the clock stops.

Full rules

Signals
Red — live signal Yellow — dead signal Grey — no signal yet
Chip reference

Each chip reads two inputs on the left and drives two outputs on the right. The four cells show every input combination. Note the family of three flippers: Top flips only the upper line, Base only the lower, Flip does both.

Rules
  1. Power sources sit on the left edge. Each one pushes a fixed red or yellow signal into its lane.
  2. Wires run left to right through empty chip slots. A slot with no chip blocks the signal — the wire stays grey.
  3. Tap a chip in the tray to pick it up, then tap a slot to drop it in. Wires recolour instantly so you can trace the result. Tap a placed chip to lift it out again, or drop a different chip on it to swap.
  4. Every lane ends at a bulb, and a bulb needs red on both of its inputs. So the goal is simply: drive every output red.
  5. The tray holds exactly the chips one solution needs, sometimes plus a spare. Every slot must end up filled, so a chip spent in the wrong place is a chip missing somewhere else.
  6. In Time Attack a placed chip is locked; use Reset to recycle the whole board.
Modes
  1. Practice — lift and swap any chip at any time. The clock runs but nothing is at stake.
  2. Time Attack — chips lock on placement, hints add 10 seconds, and your finish time goes to the leaderboard.
  3. Pass & Play — two players alternate on three identical boards. Lowest combined time wins; tied players share a rank.
  4. Expert — eight lanes, six columns, twenty-one chips. Every board is checked to make sure it cannot be solved by working left to right one slot at a time; you have to plan backwards from the outputs.
